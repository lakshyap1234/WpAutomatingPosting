# How to Choose a Home Coffee Grinder in 2026

If you've ever wondered why the coffee at your favourite café tastes better than what you make at home, the answer is usually not the beans or the machine. It's the grinder. A consistent grind is the single biggest upgrade most home brewers can make, and it doesn't have to cost a fortune.

In this guide we'll walk through the two main grinder types, what grind size you need for each brewing method, and a few models worth considering at different budgets.

![A burr coffee grinder on a kitchen counter next to a bag of whole beans](https://picsum.photos/seed/grinder-hero/1200/700 "A good burr grinder is the foundation of better coffee at home.")

## Blade vs Burr Grinders

Blade grinders work like a small blender. A spinning blade chops the beans into pieces of wildly different sizes, some almost powder and some still chunky. That mix extracts unevenly, which is why blade-ground coffee often tastes bitter and sour at the same time.

Burr grinders crush beans between two abrasive surfaces set a fixed distance apart. Every particle comes out close to the same size, so extraction is even and the flavour is cleaner. There are two kinds of burrs:

- Conical burrs: quieter, slower, retain less heat, usually cheaper
- Flat burrs: more uniform grind, favoured for espresso, usually pricier and louder

## Grind Size by Brewing Method

The right grind depends entirely on how you brew. Use this table as a starting point and adjust by taste.

| Brewing Method | Grind Size | Texture Comparison | Brew Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Turkish | Extra fine | Flour | 3-4 min |
| Espresso | Fine | Table salt | 25-30 sec |
| AeroPress | Fine-medium | Fine sand | 1-2 min |
| Pour over (V60) | Medium-fine | Coarse sand | 2.5-3.5 min |
| Drip machine | Medium | Regular sand | 4-6 min |
| French press | Coarse | Sea salt | 4 min |
| Cold brew | Extra coarse | Peppercorns | 12-24 hours |

## How to Get the Most From Your Grinder

1. Grind right before brewing. Ground coffee loses aroma within minutes.
2. Start at the manufacturer's recommended setting, then adjust one step at a time.
3. Clean the burrs every few weeks with a brush or grinder cleaning tablets.
4. Weigh your beans instead of using scoops for repeatable results.

## Final Thoughts

You don't need the most expensive grinder to taste a difference. Moving from a blade grinder to even an entry-level burr grinder will change your morning cup more than any new machine or fancy bean. Pick one that matches how you brew, keep it clean, and grind fresh every time.
